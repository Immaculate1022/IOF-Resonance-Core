# Executive Framing — Infinite Optical Fabric (IOF)

**Project Lead:** Gregory Scott Davis  
**Paradigm:** Post-von Neumann / Non-Orientable Resonant Computing  
**License:** CC BY 4.0

## Abstract (Topological Mode Convergence)

The Infinite Optical Fabric (IOF) explores a shift from the dissipative, charge-based logic of classical von Neumann architectures toward **Topological Mode Convergence**. Information is carried by light on a non-orientable (Möbius-style) photonic manifold. The design goal is to reduce the irreversible energy loss associated with linear charge transport and to treat computation as the emergence of stable resonant modes rather than sequential gate operations.

Key conceptual claims explored in the original materials:

- Replacement of linear interconnect with a continuous, closed photonic path that can recycle energy within the manifold.
- Computation as **Cognitive Resonance** — solutions appear as global standing-wave / phase-coherent patterns.
- A materials roadmap centered on Thin-Film Lithium Niobate (TFLN) waveguides, with supporting roles for graphene aerogel (energy / memory) and piezoelectric elements (actuation / triggering).

## Problem Statement (as framed)

Contemporary large-scale AI systems face an “AI Power Wall” in which a substantial fraction of energy is consumed by the physical movement of charge rather than by useful logical work. The IOF materials treat this as an architectural rather than purely engineering limitation of linear, dissipative interconnect.

## Roadmap Sketch (from original documents)

- **Phase Alpha** — Full-wave (FDTD-style) simulation of resonant manifold behavior and standing-wave stability.
- **Phase Beta** — Photonic integrated circuit exploration on TFLN, targeting high Q-factor resonators.
- **Phase Gamma** — System-level integration and energy-coherence demonstrations.

These phases remain aspirational research directions; the present repository provides open mathematical models, a real-time software resonance engine, and materials concepts so that others can examine, test, or refute the ideas.
